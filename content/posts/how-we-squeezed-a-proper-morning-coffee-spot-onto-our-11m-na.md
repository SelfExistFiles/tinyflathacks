If you live in a rented flat in the UK, you already know the drill. You get precisely three days of sun a year, and the rest of the time you are fighting a losing battle against indoor dampness while trying to dry your laundry on a rusty metal rack. 

My partner and I live in a 38m² top-floor Victorian conversion flat in South London. It has plenty of character—which is real estate agent speak for wonky floorboards, drafty windows, and a staircase so narrow that moving a mattress up here nearly ended our relationship. 

We are lucky enough to have a small outdoor space, but calling it a "balcony" is generous. It is a long, skinny strip of concrete measuring roughly 1.1 metres wide by 2.4 metres long. For two years, it sat completely unused, acting as an overflow storage zone for empty plant pots, bagged-up recycling, and our soggy clothes horse.

Every spring, I would tell myself, *"This is the year we sort out the balcony."* And every spring, I would look at garden furniture online, realise everything was either wildly expensive or built for sprawling suburban lawns, and give up. 

However, with another British summer creeping up, I decided I had had enough. I did not want a full outdoor lounge; I just wanted to sit outside on a Tuesday morning, drink my coffee properly, and pretend I was on holiday before opening my laptop for work.

### My Top Tips for Small UK Balconies:

1. **Measure the Walkway, Not Just the Space:** It’s easy to measure a balcony and think a table will fit. But if you can’t squeeze your legs past the table to sit down, it’s useless. Leave at least 45cm of clear walking space.
2. **Prioritise Free/Fast Shipping:** When buying online, watch out for sneaky delivery fees at checkout. Furniturebox including next-day UK delivery in the price makes budgeting so much easier.
3. **Choose Low-Maintenance Materials:** Powder-coated steel, glass, or synthetic rattan will survive a wet UK winter much better than untreated wood that needs sealing every spring.
4. **Keep It Light:** As a renter, you will eventually have to move. If you can't carry a piece of furniture down the stairs by yourself, don't buy it.
