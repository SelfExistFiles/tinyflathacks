I’ll be honest with you: our bathroom was a bit of a nightmare. When we first moved into this Victorian conversion, the bathroom was roughly the size of a standard broom cupboard. We’re talking 1.8 metres by 1.6 metres of pure, unadulterated chaos. If you dropped your toothbrush, it was gone forever—likely swallowed by the gap between the radiator and the basin.

For the first two years, we just lived with it. I spent most mornings performing a strange, contorted dance just to avoid hitting my elbow on the wall while brushing my teeth. It was a proper faff. Everything was cluttered, the colour scheme was a depressing "landlord beige," and honestly, the lack of storage made the whole room feel like it was closing in on us.

### The "Before" State: A Lesson in Frustration

Let’s set the scene. Imagine a space so small that if one of us was in there, the door had to stay partially shut just so the other person could stand in the hallway without getting slapped in the face. 

The storage was non-existent. We had a flimsy plastic shelf stuck to the shower screen with suction cups that failed every Tuesday without fail. Shampoos would inevitably tumble onto the floor with a clatter that sounded like a gunshot at 6:00 AM. To be fair, it was a miracle we didn’t end up with a concussion.

We spent exactly £450 on "temporary" solutions over those two years—baskets that didn’t fit, over-the-door hooks that stopped the door from closing, and those weird hanging mesh bags that just collected dust. It was money down the drain. We needed a proper overhaul, not just a bit of clever rearranging.

### The Plan: Efficiency over Aesthetics

We didn't have the budget for a complete plumbing reconfiguration—that’s a professional job, and honestly, we didn’t have the thousands of pounds for that kind of upheaval. Our plan was simple: maximise vertical space and swap out the bulky fixtures for things that actually fit.

Budget-wise, we set a limit of £800. We wanted to be smart about it. My main mission was to move everything off the floor. If it wasn’t attached to the wall or tucked away, it was clutter. 

We spent about three weeks sketching ideas on the back of a takeaway menu. The key was to replace the standard pedestal basin—which was just a glorified dust-trap—with a compact vanity unit. We also decided to swap the bulky radiator for a slimline vertical heated towel rail. It sounds small, but in a room this size, those few centimetres are the difference between "roomy" and "Claustrophobia: The Movie."

### The Execution: A Week of DIY Chaos

The renovation took us seven days. We took a week off work, which, looking back, was probably a bit optimistic. 

Taking out the old pedestal sink was a breeze, right up until I realised the pipework wasn't quite where I thought it was. That led to a frantic trip to B&Q on a rainy Sunday evening. Honestly, is it even a bathroom reno if you don’t end up soaking wet and questioning your life choices in a DIY store car park?

The most important part of the transformation was the recessed shelving. Since we couldn't go out, we had to go in. We opened up the stud wall behind the shower to create a niche. It took four hours of sweating, swearing, and accidental drywall dust inhalation, but once it was done, we had space for all our toiletries without a single suction cup in sight. 

We also painted the room a crisp, bright white. People say it’s boring, but when your bathroom is 3 square metres, you don’t want "moody." You want to be able to see your own hands.

### The "After": Breathing Room

It’s been three months since we finished, and I’m still surprised by how big it feels. We installed a wall-mounted vanity unit, which creates a visual "floating" effect that makes the floor space seem much larger. 

By replacing the pedestal with a vanity, we gained enough room to store all our cleaning supplies underneath, which means they are no longer sitting out on the counter. The new vertical towel rail provides heat while occupying only a fraction of the wall space the old radiator used. 

We also added a mirrored cabinet. It’s a classic move, I know, but for a reason. It doubles our storage and bounces light around the room, making it feel less like a box and more like a proper sanctuary. 

Total cost? £764. We came in under budget by £36, which went straight into a very necessary celebratory curry.

### Final Thoughts: Was it Worth the Faff?

Looking back, was it a nightmare? Sometimes. There were moments when we were scrubbing grout at 11:00 PM and wondering why we didn't just move house. But having a bathroom where I can actually reach my moisturiser without performing a gymnastics routine is, quite honestly, life-changing.

If you’re living in a tiny flat and feeling crushed by your bathroom, my advice is to stop buying storage hacks that just take up more room. Look at your walls. Look at your vanity. If it’s sitting on the floor and it doesn’t need to be, move it. If you can hide it behind a mirror, do it.

It’s not about having a grand, Instagram-worthy spa. It’s about having a space that works for you, so you don't start your morning feeling like you’re losing a wrestling match with your own furniture. 

Now, if you’ll excuse me, I’m off to enjoy a shower without the fear of falling shampoo bottles. It’s the little things, isn’t it?
