Right, let's be honest, hands up if your 'living room' is actually a multi-functional chaos zone where your laptop battles for space with your half-eaten dinner, and your comfy sofa doubles as a very expensive laundry basket? ### The Cluttered Nightmare: Before the Big Sort Out

Picture this: a decent-sized room, but only just. Our flat, like many here in the city, has its charm – original floorboards, a lovely bay window – but generous proportions it is *not*. When we both started working from home more regularly, our previously cosy living room transformed into a battleground of screens, chargers, paperwork, and discarded coffee cups.

![Before Makeover](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555041469-a586c61ea9bc?w=800)

Our old, hand-me-down sofa – bless its cotton socks – was huge and took up most of one wall, leaving little room for anything else. We had a rickety old desk crammed into a corner, piled high with Liam's tech bits and my various notebooks. There was no real definition, no sense of 'work is done now, time to chill'. It was all just... there. All the time.

Honestly, it was a bloody nightmare. Trying to relax in the evening with a half-finished spreadsheet staring at you from across the room is hardly conducive to peace and quiet, is it? We'd sit there, trying to watch telly, but our eyes would keep drifting to the pile of post on the coffee table (which was also our dining table, let's be real), or the tangle of wires behind the 'work' corner. It made the whole flat feel smaller, messier, and much more stressful than it needed to be.

We knew we needed a proper solution. Something that would allow us to actually *switch off* at the end of the day, but without having to drag our work stuff into the bedroom (definitely not an option – our bedroom is even smaller!). We needed to define zones, create boundaries, and reclaim our living room for, well, living!

**2. The Flexible Work Zone: Lucia Nest of Tables**

Now, for the work area. We couldn't dedicate a permanent desk, so we needed something versatile. The **Lucia Nest of Tables** was our absolute stroke of genius here. We positioned them next to the window, opposite the sofa. This set comes with three tables that slot neatly inside each other, taking up minimal floor space when not in use.

When Liam or I need to work, we simply pull out one or two of the tables. They're lightweight enough to move around easily, yet sturdy enough for a laptop, a mug of tea, and some notes. It's proper useful for guests too, as extra surfaces for drinks and nibbles. This instantly tidies up the room and creates a mental distinction: tables nested = work off; tables pulled out = work on.
