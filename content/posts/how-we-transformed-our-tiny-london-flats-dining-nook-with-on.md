Honestly, if you're a renter in London (or Manchester, or Bristol – anywhere with properly compact flats, let's be fair), you know the struggle is real. My partner, Liam, and I have been in our two-bed flat near Clapham for three years now, and while we absolutely adore the area, the flat itself has always been… a *bit* of a Tetris challenge. Our living room, bless its cotton socks, was meant to be a lounge *and* a dining area. In reality, it was mostly a lounge that occasionally hosted a very awkward, wobbly fold-out table from our student days, surrounded by a mountain of "stuff we don't know where else to put."

### Intro & Before: The Cluttered Nightmare

For ages, our dining "experience" consisted of either perching on the sofa with plates on our laps – risking a good dollop of spaghetti bolognese on Liam's favourite (and only) white t-shirt – or, if we were feeling fancy, dragging out that aforementioned table. You know the type: it had more splinters than stability, and took a good fifteen minutes of faffing about just to get it upright. Then, once erected (charming, I know), it would block the pathway to the kitchen, making any culinary effort a proper obstacle course.

The "dining corner" itself was just a forgotten alcove, a magnet for anything homeless. We're talking gym bags, a pile of unopened mail, some rather sad-looking houseplants, and a tower of board games we rarely played because, frankly, there was never any surface big enough to spread them out on. It was a proper jumble sale, a depressing, neglected space that just sucked the joy out of what should have been a cosy corner.

When friends came over, it was even worse. We’d try to squeeze four people around that rickety table, knees knocking, elbows clashing, and someone always ended up having to balance their drink precariously on the armrest of the sofa. It was less "dinner party" and more "extreme sport." We’d been meaning to sort it out for ages, but the thought of trying to find something that would actually *fit* our tiny space, look decent, and not cost an arm and a leg, felt like a bloody nightmare.
