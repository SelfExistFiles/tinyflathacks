Alright, fellow tiny flat dwellers! Let's be honest, living in a British city – whether it's London, Manchester, or like me, here in good old Bristol – often means making peace with a serious lack of space. Our two-bed rental in Cliftonwood might have stunning views of the Suspension Bridge (on a clear day, mind), but inside, it's a bit more "cosy" than "spacious." Especially when it comes to the dreaded dining situation.

For ages, our so-called 'dining corner' was less a place for delicious meals and more a multi-purpose dumping ground. It was a holding bay for anything that didn't immediately have a home: a laundry basket full of clean (but never folded) clothes, my partner's ever-growing pile of graphic novels, a rogue charging cable, and the occasional forgotten takeaway container. Eating a proper meal at a *table* felt like a luxury reserved for holidays or our parents' houses. Most nights, it was balancing plates on laps on the sofa, trying not to spill Bolognese down the cushions. Honestly, it was a bloody nightmare.

Trying to host more than one other couple? Forget about it. We’d end up perched on kitchen stools, balancing drinks, feeling like we were playing a giant game of musical chairs. Something had to give. We needed to reclaim that corner and turn it into a functional, inviting space.
