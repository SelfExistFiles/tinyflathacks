Right, let me tell you—if you’ve ever lived in a standard British flat, you know the pain of the "shoebox bathroom." Ours was, quite frankly, a bit of a nightmare. It measured about 1.8 metres by 2.2 metres, and honestly, you couldn't swing a cat in there without hitting the towel rail or knocking over a shampoo bottle.

For three years, I lived with a bathroom that felt like it was shrinking every time I walked in. Every surface was cluttered, the tiles were a questionable shade of 90s cream, and the storage situation? Let's just say my skincare routine was mostly living in a plastic crate on the floor. It was a proper faff just to find a clean towel without creating an avalanche of toiletries.

### The "Before" – A Total Disaster

To be fair, it wasn't *technically* falling apart, but it was just so inefficient. We had a bulky vanity unit that took up half the floor space but offered zero actual storage because the sink plumbing hogged all the cupboard space inside. 

I’ll be honest with you, I spent months just staring at the layout, promising myself I’d fix it. I watched countless DIY videos, ordered things that never arrived, and then spent three weeks debating whether to change the tiles. In the end, procrastination won until I accidentally knocked over a bottle of expensive body wash and it shattered all over the floor. That was the breaking point. 

The room was 3.9 square metres of wasted potential. Everything was shoved onto the edges of the sink, and the damp smell of those cheap plastic storage boxes was starting to get on my nerves. It was time to stop faffing about and get the tools out.

### The Plan – Less is More (But Better)

We had a budget of £350, which is tight, but I’m a stubborn DIYer. The goal wasn't a total rip-out (we’re renting, for heaven’s sake, and we don't have the budget to redo the plumbing), but rather a clever "soft" renovation.

Here is what we did:

1. **The Mirror Cabinet Swap:** We swapped our flat mirror for a recessed-look cabinet. Okay, we couldn't cut into the wall, so we found a shallow, slimline cabinet that didn't stick out too far. It cost £85. It fits right above the sink and hides all those unsightly toothpaste tubes and daily essentials.
2. **Vertical Shelf Hack:** We added three floating shelves above the toilet. It sounds like a basic move, but using the dead air space changed everything. We bought some reclaimed wood for £30 and used heavy-duty brackets. It’s amazing how much "stuff" you can store when you actually go upwards.
3. **Over-the-Door Hooks:** I know, it’s not rocket science, but those cheap metal hooks were a game-changer. We installed four of them for £12. No more towels draped over the radiator looking like a damp, sad laundry pile.
4. **The Paint Job:** We spent £45 on a high-quality, mould-resistant paint in a crisp white. It made the room feel double the size. I’ll be honest, the masking tape part was a total nightmare—I think I spent three hours just making sure I wouldn't paint the ceiling by accident.

### The "Faff" Along the Way

Of course, nothing ever goes to plan. I’ll be the first to admit that I’m not a professional builder. When we started drilling for the shelves, I hit a pipe. Well, not a pipe, but a bit of the wall that crumbled like a digestive biscuit. It turned a two-hour job into a weekend-long project involving fillers, sanding, and a lot of swearing.

I also miscalculated the depth of the cabinet. I bought one that was 20cm deep, thinking it would be fine. Turns out, it hit my head every time I leaned over the sink. That was a proper mistake. We had to take it down, return it, and find a 12cm deep one instead. Honestly, measure twice, cut once—or in my case, measure four times and still feel like an idiot.

But there is something incredibly satisfying about standing back after the chaos and seeing a clean, organised space.

### The "After" – A Different World

It’s been two months since we finished, and I’m still buzzing about it. The room feels twice as big simply because the floor is clear. Being able to actually *find* my razor without shifting five other items is a small victory, but it makes the morning rush so much easier.

We spent a total of £245—well under our £350 limit. The rest went towards some fancy baskets to keep things tidy on the shelves, which makes the whole place feel a bit more like a spa and less like a public loo.

The white walls and the clever storage have turned this tiny, cramped room into something that is actually quite pleasant to be in. It’s not an Instagram-worthy mansion bathroom, but it’s ours, it works, and it’s finally, properly organised.

### A Few Bits of Advice

If you’re sitting there in a tiny flat, feeling like you’re losing the battle against your own bathroom, here’s my takeaway:

- **Look Up:** You’d be surprised how much storage you’re missing out on just because you’re ignoring the space above the toilet or the door.
- **Ditch the Bulk:** If your vanity unit is rubbish, get rid of it. Swap it for a pedestal sink or a slimline one and use wall-mounted storage instead. It opens up the floor, which makes the room feel much larger.
- **Don't Rush the Prep:** I know I wanted to get to the "finished" bit as fast as possible, but the time spent sanding and priming is what makes the job last. Don't be like me and try to rush it, or you’ll be re-painting in six months.
- **Embrace the Imperfections:** Things will go wrong. You will drop a screw down the drain. You will buy the wrong size shelf. Just laugh about it, have a cuppa, and get back to it.

Renovating a tiny space isn’t about making it perfect; it’s about making it work for you. And honestly? It’s the best £245 we’ve spent on this flat. Now, if I could just figure out how to stop the shower curtain from sticking to my legs, I’d be sorted. But that’s a project for another day!
