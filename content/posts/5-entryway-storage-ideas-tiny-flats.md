Okay, let's be honest – when your flat is tiny, the entryway is usually the last place you think about. You just want somewhere to dump your keys and kick off your shoes, right?

But here's the thing. A messy entryway makes the whole flat feel chaotic the second you walk in. And if you live in a UK flat with no proper hallway (which is like 90% of us), you know the struggle is real.

So I've been testing out a few ideas in my own flat, and here are 5 that actually work without making your entrance feel crammed.

---

## 1. Wall-Mounted Hooks + Shelf Combo

This is the MVP of entryway storage. Literally anyone can do this – you don't even need to drill if you use command strips or over-door hooks.

I got a simple wooden shelf with 3 hooks underneath from IKEA (the TJUSIG series, if you're curious). It took maybe 5 minutes to put up.

**What it holds:**
- Shelf: keys, mail, sunglasses, random pocket stuff
- Hooks: coats, bags, umbrellas – you know, the usual

**Why it works:** Zero floor space taken up. It's all on the wall.

## 2. A Slim Bench with Storage Inside

This one's been a total game changer for me. I used to just drop my bag on the floor by the door, which looked a mess and was a tripping hazard.

Then I found this narrow wooden bench that's only about 35cm deep. It fits perfectly against the wall next to my front door. The top is for sitting on while I put on shoes, and the lid lifts up to reveal storage underneath.

**What goes inside:**
- Shoes I don't wear every day (guest slippers, summer sandals, boots in winter)
- Scarves and gloves
- A spare umbrella

**Pro tip:** Measure the depth of your entryway *before* you buy anything. I learned this the hard way – bought something that was too deep and ended up returning it.

## 3. Over-Door Organisers

Okay, this one sounds a bit basic, but hear me out.

Over-door shoe organisers aren't just for shoes. I use one with clear pockets on the back of my front door, and it's brilliant.

| Pocket | What's in it |
|--------|--------------|
| Top row | Sunglasses, hand sanitiser, lip balm |
| Middle row | Post, spare keys, dog lead |
| Bottom row | Umbrella, reusable shopping bags, masks |

The best part? It costs like £5 and takes 2 minutes to hang. No tools needed. Perfect for rental flats where you can't make permanent changes.

**[Find over-door organisers on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=over+door+organiser)**

## 4. A Mirror That Actually Does Something

If you're going to have a mirror in your entryway (which you probably should – need to check your hair before leaving), why not get one that works as storage too?

There are these clever mirrors that fold out to reveal hidden hooks or a small cabinet behind them.

**Why I love this:**
- Looks like a normal mirror – doesn't scream "storage solution"
- Hides mess instantly when you close it
- Great for small spaces because it serves two purposes

**[Shop storage mirrors →](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=storage+mirror+hallway)**

## 5. Baskets on the Floor (But Make It Neat)

Okay, this one's basically cheating because it's so simple. Just get 2-3 matching baskets and put them on the floor.

**What it solves:** The pile of shoes that keeps growing by the door. And the "where do I put this?" chaos.

I use two woven baskets – one for shoes, one for things that need to go out (like returns, library books, stuff to lend to friends). It looks intentional rather than messy.

**My tip:** Get baskets with lids if your entryway is visible from your living area – it's much tidier that way.
