If you’re anything like us – a couple of lifelong renters trying to make a tiny flat in a bustling city (Bristol, in our case) feel like a proper home – you’ll know the struggle. We’ve managed to hack our living room, squeeze a dining nook into the kitchen, and even make our bedroom feel a bit more spacious than it has any right to be. But there was one area that, honestly, had become a bit of a neglected nightmare: our 'balcony'.

I use the term 'balcony' loosely, because what we actually had was more of a glorified ledge. It was about 1.5 metres long and just shy of a metre deep. Barely enough room to swing a cat, let alone enjoy a relaxing cup of tea. For months – well, let’s be real, for *years* – it had served as a dumping ground for forgotten plant pots, a rusty old BBQ that hadn't seen a flame since our first summer here, and a collection of random tools that my partner swore he’d ‘get round to using’.

It was a sad sight, and a proper waste of what little outdoor space we actually possessed. Every sunny morning (and yes, we do get a few of those in Britain, honest!) I’d look at it and dream of a tiny table, a couple of chairs, and the simple pleasure of sipping my coffee whilst breathing in some fresh, albeit city-tinged, air. Instead, I’d be dodging cobwebs and trying not to trip over the mouldy remains of a defunct watering can.

We’d tried. Oh, we’d tried. Once, we even bought a cheap plastic folding chair, but it was so flimsy it felt like a strong gust of wind would send it (and me) plummeting to the street below. Plus, it took up practically the entire width, making navigation a bloody nightmare. Most of the time, the space was just... there. An empty, cluttered testament to our procrastination.

My partner, bless him, had optimistically bought a couple of herbs which, after a week of neglect, had withered into sad, crispy relics. They were still sitting in their plastic pots, long past their sell-by date, adding to the general air of desolation. It wasn't just an eyesore; it was a barrier to those simple pleasures, like enjoying the occasional bit of sunshine with a proper cuppa. Every time I looked out there, I just sighed. It felt like a problem too small to bother with, yet too big to ignore.
