Right, let’s settle this.

If you live in a small flat in the UK and you’ve ever tried to buy furniture, you’ve probably had this exact internal debate: **IKEA or something else?**

IKEA is the obvious choice. It’s everywhere. Everyone’s been there. You’ve got a blue bag somewhere in your flat. But then you see brands like Furniturebox popping up – similar prices, similar styles, but they promise next-day delivery and slightly less assembly drama.

I’ve bought from both over the last couple of years. So here’s my honest, no-nonsense comparison.

---

## The Quick Summary

| | IKEA | Furniturebox |
|---|------|--------------|
| **Price** | Cheap, but adds up with delivery | Mid-range, often cheaper than you'd think |
| **Quality** | Fine – some stuff is great, some is proper rubbish | Consistently decent – no massive surprises |
| **Assembly** | You'll be at it for hours | Usually comes pre-assembled or very easy to put together |
| **Delivery** | Costs extra, can take ages | Free next-day (if you order before 8pm) |
| **Range** | Huge – you can get everything | Smaller, but well-curated |
| **Flat-pack dread** | Very high | Low |

---

## Price: Where’s Your Money Going?

**IKEA:** You can get a table for £30. You can also get a table for £300. The range is massive, which is both a blessing and a curse. The cheap stuff is genuinely cheap – but you get what you pay for. Also, delivery is usually about £25–£40 unless you live near a store and can collect.

**Furniturebox:** Prices sit in the middle. You won’t find a £30 table, but you also won’t find anything that feels like it’ll fall apart if you look at it wrong. The big win is **free next-day delivery** – which, honestly, is a game changer when you’re impatient (like me) or you’ve just moved in and have nowhere to sit.

**Verdict:** If you’re on an absolute shoestring budget, IKEA wins. But if you can stretch a tiny bit more, Furniturebox is often better value when you factor in delivery.

---

## Quality: What Actually Lasts?

**IKEA:** It’s a mixed bag. Billy bookcases are genuinely great. So are their kitchen cabinets. But some of the cheaper stuff – like the £15 side tables – have a habit of wobbling after a few months. I’ve had a LACK table that survived four flat moves, and another one that fell apart after six months. Bit of a lottery, honestly.

**Furniturebox:** Everything I’ve bought from them feels consistent. It’s not luxury furniture – it’s not made of solid oak or whatever – but it’s sturdy enough. The drop-leaf desk I’ve got has proper metal hinges. The storage bench doesn’t wobble. It feels like they’ve actually tested the stuff before selling it.

**Verdict:** Furniturebox is more consistent. IKEA has higher highs and lower lows.

---

## Assembly: The Real Test of Patience

**IKEA:** Look, we all know the drill. You open the box, you see a million pieces, you lose one of the little wooden dowels, you spend two hours swearing at the instructions, and eventually you end up with a piece of furniture that’s maybe 90% correct.

I’m not saying I’ve ever cried over an IKEA assembly. But I’ve come close.

**Furniturebox:** Most of their stuff comes pre-assembled or requires very minimal assembly – like attaching legs or screwing on a handle. The drop-leaf desk I ordered took maybe 5 minutes to put together. No swearing. No leftover screws (that I’ve found so far).

**Verdict:** This is the biggest difference. If you hate assembly (like me), Furniturebox wins hands down.

---

## Delivery: The Deciding Factor

**IKEA:** This is where IKEA properly falls down. Delivery can take a week or more, and it costs extra. If you live near a store, fine – you can collect. But if you’re ordering online, be prepared to wait and to pay.

**Furniturebox:** Free next-day delivery if you order before 8pm on a weekday. I’ve used this a few times now and it’s always arrived when they said it would. For a small flat dweller, this is massive – you can order something and have it the next day without having to take a day off work to wait in.

**Verdict:** Furniturebox, and it’s not even close.

---

## Range: Who Has More Choice?

**IKEA:** Obviously IKEA. You can furnish an entire flat from one trip. Sofas, beds, kitchens, lamps, plants, candles, even tea towels. They cover everything.

**Furniturebox:** They’re more focused – living, dining, bedroom, home office. You won’t find kitchen cabinets or lighting here. But for the core stuff, they’ve got a decent selection, and the range feels curated rather than overwhelming.

**Verdict:** IKEA for range. Furniturebox for less decision fatigue.

---

## So, Which One’s Actually Better for a Tiny Flat?

It depends on what you need.

**Go IKEA if:**
- You’re on a proper tight budget
- You don’t mind assembly and actually enjoy the process (weird, but fine)
- You need a specific piece that only they do
- You live near a store and can collect in person

**Go Furniturebox if:**
- You hate flat-pack and want minimal assembly
- You want free next-day delivery without the faff
- You’re after decent quality without paying premium prices
- You want to buy something and just have it work, with no surprises

---

## What I’d Do Differently

In hindsight, I’d have bought more from Furniturebox earlier.

I’ve got a few IKEA pieces that are still going strong – the usual bookcases, some storage cubes, a few bits that have lasted years. But I’ve also got a few IKEA pieces that I got rid of after one move because they fell to bits.

Furniturebox is more reliable for the stuff you actually care about. Their foldable desk, storage bench, and ottoman are all still in use. No wobbling. No regrets.

If you want the full IKEA experience – the meatballs, the maze, the blue bags – go to IKEA. If you just want furniture delivered quickly that works without you building a career in flat-pack assembly, go Furniturebox.

Honestly? I’ve got a mix of both in my flat now – and I’m fine with that.
