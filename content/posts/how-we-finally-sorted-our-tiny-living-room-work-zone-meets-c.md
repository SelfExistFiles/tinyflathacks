Alright, fellow small-space dwellers, let me tell you a tale as old as time (or at least as old as working from home in a poky city flat). My partner and I live in a lovely, but undeniably *compact*, two-bedroom flat in South London. When we first moved in, the living room felt like a proper cosy sanctuary – all mismatched cushions, fairy lights, and dreams of weekend Netflix binges. Then, 2020 happened, and suddenly that "cosy sanctuary" became a full-time office, a dining room, a gym (briefly, let’s be honest), and still somehow, a place to relax. It was, to put it mildly, a bit of a nightmare.

### Intro & Before: The Cluttered Nightmare

![Before Makeover](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555041469-a586c61ea9bc?w=800)

Imagine this: a tiny, almost square-shaped room. Our old, chunky sofa took up most of one wall. Opposite it, a TV unit that doubled as a bookshelf, a plant stand, and a dumping ground for whatever arrived through the post. The "dining table" was a cheap, wobbly affair that spent more time pushed against a wall than actually being used for eating.

My workspace? A laptop precariously balanced on a cushion on the sofa, or worse, on that wobbly dining table, forcing us to eat dinner with our plates on our laps. My partner had a slightly better setup in the spare room, but we often ended up side-by-side in the living room, trying to concentrate while the other was on a video call. Our "relaxation" area was, by necessity, also our "work" area, our "eating" area, and our "where did I put that remote?" area.

There were laptops, chargers, notebooks, coffee mugs (often forgotten and cold), and various bits of paperwork strewn everywhere. My back ached, my eyes were constantly tired from staring at a screen from the wrong angle, and honestly, the mental clutter was worse than the physical. I'd finish work, look up from my laptop, and still feel like I was *in* my office. There was no clear boundary, no moment of truly switching off. We'd try to watch a film, but the work emails were still pinging, the work phone still sitting mockingly beside the snacks. It was doing my head in.

We knew something had to give. We needed to carve out distinct zones: a proper place to work without feeling like we were colonising the entire flat, and a genuinely relaxing space where we could unwind without seeing the ghosts of spreadsheets past. But how do you do that in a living room that’s barely bigger than a postage stamp?
