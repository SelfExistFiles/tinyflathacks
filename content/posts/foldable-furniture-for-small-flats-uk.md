I'll be honest – I used to think foldable furniture was proper naff. You know, the kind of thing your nan keeps in the shed for when the family descends at Christmas. Not something you'd actually *want* in your living space.

Then I moved into a flat in London where the "second bedroom" was basically a cupboard with a window. And suddenly, furniture that folds, collapses, or hides itself started looking very attractive indeed.

Here's what I've learned, what I bought, and what I actually use.

---

## The thing about small flats

If you live in one, you already know the struggle. You walk into a room and you can see *everything* at once. There's no hiding. Every piece of furniture is on full display, so it had better earn its keep.

A big bulky sofa that seats four? Great if you host dinner parties every week. Useless if you live alone and it just sits there, taking up floor space you could actually use.

That's where foldable stuff comes in.

---

I got a wall-mounted drop-leaf desk from [Furniturebox](https://www.furniturebox.co.uk/desks) – nothing fancy, just a wooden board on a hinge with a fold-out leg.

If you work from home even a couple of days a week, one of these is genuinely life-changing.

## What I'd Say to Someone Thinking About Foldable Furniture

Honestly, do it. But manage your expectations.

Foldable furniture is a compromise. It's not as solid or as permanent as the real thing. If you're after heirloom-quality furniture that'll last 50 years, this isn't it.

But if you live in a small flat and you're fed up with not having space to move? Foldable stuff is a game changer.

The trick is:
- Buy for your *actual* life, not your ideal life
- Don't go too cheap – mechanisms matter
- Measure your space twice, order once

I've had a few foldable things break on me (cheap hinges, dodgy locks). The ones that work well are the ones where I spent a bit more – like the Furniturebox drop-leaf desk, which has proper metal hinges and a leg that locks into place properly.
