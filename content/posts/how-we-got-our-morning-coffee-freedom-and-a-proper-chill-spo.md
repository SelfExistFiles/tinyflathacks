Alright, fellow tiny flat dwellers! Let me paint you a picture. It’s a grey, drizzly Tuesday morning in London (shocking, I know), and all I want is to start my day with a proper cuppa, breathing in some *actual* fresh air that isn't filtered through our kitchen window. The dream, right? A cosy little spot, a bit of greenery, the gentle hum of the city waking up. Pure bliss.

The reality? Well, let’s just say our "outdoor space" was less a tranquil haven and more a sad, cluttered afterthought.

### Before: The Cluttered Nightmare

For ages, our tiny balcony had been, honestly, a proper nightmare. Living in a rented flat in Hackney, outdoor space is a luxury, even if it's barely bigger than a postage stamp. We’d inherited a couple of knackered old plastic chairs from the previous tenants, one of which was cracked and perpetually damp, and a small, wobbly metal table that probably started life as something you'd see outside a greasy spoon.

It became a dumping ground. An old laundry airer that never quite folded away properly, a few dead plant pots from a failed gardening experiment (RIP basil), my flatmate’s rarely-used bike, and a general assortment of 'things we might need someday'. Every time I opened the patio door, I’d be met with this rather depressing sight. It wasn't just untidy; it was a constant reminder of unused potential.

We’d occasionally brave it, squeezing onto the least-damaged plastic chair with our mugs, trying to ignore the clutter, but it was hardly the serene morning ritual I craved. There was no room to properly stretch out, no space to really relax, and certainly no warmth or inviting vibe. It was an obstacle course, a storage unit, anything but a place to enjoy our morning brew. Honestly, it was a bit of a faff just trying to get out there.

Plus, anything decent seemed to cost an arm and a leg, which, let's face it, isn't exactly renter-friendly. We needed a proper solution, something that wouldn't break the bank or require a full-scale renovation (not allowed anyway, obviously).
