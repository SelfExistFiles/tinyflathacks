When you're renting in the UK, you learn pretty quickly that furniture is a pain.

You can't drill into walls for proper shelves (or at least, you're not supposed to). You can't swap out the landlord's terrible built-in wardrobe. And you definitely don't want to buy a massive three-seater sofa that you'll have to drag up three flights of stairs when you inevitably move flats next year.

I've been renting for about six years now, and I've gone through a fair few "furniture phases." The bulky phase, where I bought stuff that looked nice but was a nightmare to move. The cheap IKEA phase, where everything fell apart after one flat move. And finally, the flexible phase – where I only buy stuff that adapts, folds, or packs down.

Here's what I've kept.

---

This might sound obvious, but I genuinely didn't think of it for ages.

I needed a sideboard for my living room. I also needed a desk for when I work from home. I didn't have space for both. So I found a sideboard that's the right height for a desk – about 75cm tall – and now it does both jobs.

**How it works:**
- Normal day: it holds my TV, a lamp, and some books
- Work day: I clear the top, set up my laptop, and use it as a standing desk

**The bonus:** It's got storage underneath for all my cables, notebooks, and miscellaneous crap that used to live in a box by the sofa.

I know it's not technically "folding" furniture, but it's flexible in a different way – and it saves me from having to cram a separate desk into my already crowded living room.

## 4. Chair That Folds Flat Behind the Sofa


This one's simple but properly useful.

I keep a folding wooden chair tucked behind my sofa. Nobody can see it from the front – it's completely hidden. When I have an extra guest over, I pull it out, unfold it, and suddenly there's another seat.

**Why I love it:**
- It takes up basically zero floor space
- It unfolds in two seconds
- It's genuinely comfortable for a couple of hours (I've tested it)

**The catch:** It doesn't look as nice as my proper dining chairs. But it's hidden most of the time, so I don't really care.

If you've got even a tiny gap behind a sofa or next to a wardrobe, a folding chair is a no-brainer.

## What I've Learned About Furniture for Renters

If you're renting a small flat in the UK, here's what I wish someone had told me earlier:

**Measure absolutely everything.** I can't stress this enough. I bought a chest of drawers once without measuring the corridor and ended up having to return it – proper faff.

**Think about how you'll move it.** If you can't carry it up a narrow staircase by yourself, maybe don't buy it.

**Buy for your actual life.** If you never host dinner parties, you don't need a massive extendable dining table. But if you work from home and eat dinner on your lap, maybe you do need that drop-leaf desk after all.
