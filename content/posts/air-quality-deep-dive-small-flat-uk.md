Right, let's talk about something that doesn't get enough attention.

If you live in a small flat, you've probably noticed it gets stuffy quickly. You cook dinner and the smell lingers for hours. You wake up with a dry throat. Your windows steam up in winter. You dust on Saturday and by Wednesday there's already a layer of grey back on the shelves.

I used to think this was just "part of flat life". Turns out, it's not. It's a sign that your indoor air quality is out of whack. And it can actually affect how you feel – day to day, week to week.

So I spent two months properly looking into this. I tested solutions, measured results, and kept notes. Here's what I found.

---

## The Before: The Science of Stuffy

Before I did anything, I wanted to understand *why* my flat felt stuffy. So I got a few basic tools and started measuring.

![Dusty air vent and condensation on window](/image_873475557800810.webp)

**The measurements (pre-makeover):**

| Metric | My Reading | Ideal Range |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| Humidity | 72-78% | 40-60% |
| CO2 (after 8 hours sleeping) | ~1,200 ppm | < 800 ppm |
| Dust particles | High (visible within 2 days) | Low |

**The problems (why small flats are worse):**
- **Less volume of air**: Same amount of pollutants in a smaller space = higher concentration.
- **Poor ventilation**: Many UK flats have limited air circulation, especially if you're on a higher floor or have windows on one side only.
- **More surface area per volume**: More walls, more fabric, more places for dust to settle.
- **Cooking and drying clothes**: These produce moisture and particles that have nowhere to go.

**How it actually made me feel:**
- Waking up with a dry throat and slightly groggy (most mornings)
- Feeling a bit "heavy" after a few hours indoors
- Noticing a stale smell that I'd just got used to
- Headaches occasionally – not severe, but there

I didn't realise all of this was connected until I started measuring.

## The After: Systematically Fixing It

I didn't just buy one thing and hope for the best. I approached it like a mini project. Each change solved a specific problem.

![Clean room with air purifier and clear window](/image_873475557800810.webp)

Here's the exact setup I ended up with, and why each piece matters.

### 1. Air Purifier (The Big One)
This is the main investment. I got a [Blueair purifier](https://www.blueair.co.uk) designed for rooms up to about 25sqm – basically the size of my whole flat.

**What it actually does:**
- Draws in air, passes it through a filter, traps particles (dust, pollen, smoke, mould spores)
- The filter I use captures 99.97% of particles down to 0.3 microns
- It runs quietly enough that I don't notice it

**The real-world impact:**
- Dust settles *much* slower – I noticed this within the first week
- The morning grogginess faded after a few days
- My flat smells neutral now, not "trapped"

### 2. Humidity Monitor + Dehumidifier (Second Major Fix)
My flat was sitting at 72-78% humidity. That's not just uncomfortable – it's a breeding ground for dust mites and mould.

I got a small dehumidifier and run it for a couple of hours a day, especially when I'm drying clothes.

**The impact:**
- Humidity dropped to 52-58% within a week
- Windows stopped steaming up
- The air feels lighter, not heavy

### 3. Ventilation Routine (Free Fix)
This one cost nothing but took discipline.

I now open windows for 15 minutes every morning, even in winter. It sounds simple, but it flushes out the CO2 that builds up overnight.

**The impact:**
- CO2 levels dropped from ~1,200 ppm to ~650 ppm within 30 minutes
- I feel more alert in the mornings

### 4. Foldable Drying Rack (Cheap Fix)
I used to dry clothes on the radiator, which pumps moisture into the air. Now I use a foldable rack near the window and open the window for 20 minutes when drying.

**The impact:**
- Humidity spike from drying clothes is now minimal
- Clothes dry in about the same time, but the flat stays drier

### 5. Houseplants (The Nice Fix)
Not a massive impact, but they help slightly and they make the space feel better. I got a spider plant, a peace lily, and a snake plant.

**The impact:**
- Small improvement in humidity regulation
- The flat feels calmer and more pleasant

In order of impact:
